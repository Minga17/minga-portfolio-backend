WHO
01 Introduction

Who I Am

I was born in the Democratic Republic of Congo and moved to Zimbabwe when I was nine years old. Zimbabwe is where I spent the biggest part of my life. It is where I grew up, where I made friends, where I learned how to work hard, and where I became the person I am today.

In my third year of high school, something clicked. I realised I wanted to build a career in IT. The problem was I did not know which part. There are so many directions you can go in technology and at that age, none of them had a clear sign pointing at me saying this is the one.

When my family moved to the United States, I started college. I was showing up, taking classes, doing the work. But if I am honest, I was going through the motions. Nothing was pulling me in strongly enough. I was in the building but I had not found my room yet.

Then one conversation changed everything. I was talking to my boss one day about where I wanted to go with my career. He noticed I was good with numbers and that I had a strong ability to learn quickly. He suggested IT development. I appreciated it. But when I sat with it, something still did not feel completely right.

So I went home and did my own research. I went through different areas of IT one by one until I found two that stopped me in my tracks. Data Analytics and Data Engineering. Both of them made sense to me in a way nothing else had before. They combined the things I was naturally drawn to, working with numbers, finding patterns, solving problems, and building something real from raw information.

My plan from that moment has been clear. Short term, build strong foundations in Data Analytics. Long term, become a Data Engineer. Those are not random goals. They are connected. Data Analytics teaches you how to understand data. Data Engineering teaches you how to build the systems that move it. You need one to truly master the other.

Right now I am fully focused on that first goal. Every project I build, every dataset I clean, every insight I find is a step toward it.

02 Personal Background

Where I'm From

🇨🇩 The Democratic Republic of Congo gave me my origin. 🇿🇼 Zimbabwe gave me my foundation. 🇺🇸 The United States is giving me my future.

Moving to Zimbabwe at nine years old was not easy. But looking back, it was the place that shaped me the most. Zimbabwe taught me things no classroom ever could.

It taught me to take from your environment and give back to it. To be part of something bigger than yourself. To look around at your community and ask what you can contribute, not just what you can take.

It taught me to appreciate what I have. When you grow up watching people around you survive on very little, you stop taking things for granted. You learn to cherish the small things. You learn that enough is actually a lot.

Zimbabwe is also where I fell in love with sport and music. Football taught me discipline, teamwork and how to read a situation fast. Music taught me patience and how to find structure inside something that feels like chaos. Both of those things show up in how I approach data today.

And Zimbabwe taught me how to survive hard times. There were seasons where school fees were a real struggle. Seasons where motivation was nowhere to be found. Seasons where keeping going felt like the hardest thing in the world. I kept going anyway. That is not something you learn from a book. That is something life teaches you by putting you through it.

Every environment I have lived in has left something in me. The DRC gave me roots. Zimbabwe gave me strength. America is giving me a canvas to build on.
